# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary reader is the teacher grading the submission: a three-member group ePortfolio for a community/social-studies course with a philosophy focus (group name: PETA). Classmates may also view it. The teacher is evaluating whether the group's community engagement, interviews, and reflections are genuine and thoughtfully worked through, not just completed for a checklist.

## Product Purpose

This ePortfolio documents a group's community engagement project — the background, the people interviewed, and the philosophical and personal reflection that followed — for course assessment. It exists to give the reflection a home that reads as considered and sincere rather than a bare compliance document. Success looks like a teacher (and classmates) coming away convinced the group actually engaged with the community and thought seriously about what it meant, not that they filled in a template.

## Positioning

Every page proves genuine, thoughtful community engagement — the group showed up, listened, and reflected honestly, rather than going through the motions.

## Brand Personality

Sincere, warm, and contemplative, with enough polish to read as credible academic work. The voice is personal and reflective, not corporate or promotional — first-person where it fits, specific over generic, calm rather than showy.

## Anti-references

Not a generic corporate template or SaaS-landing-page feel (no hero-metric blocks, no stock-photo gloss, no salesy CTAs). Avoid garish color or gimmicky decoration that would undercut sincerity, and avoid a tone that reads flippant or performative — the reflection should feel earned.

## Design Principles

Show, don't tell: let real excerpts from interviews and reflections carry the weight, not decorative filler around them. Document-like calm: editorial pacing and generous white space over app-like density or card-grid scaffolding. Warmth without whimsy: human warmth comes through tone, typography, and structure, not playful gimmicks. Credibility through craft: typographic hierarchy and layout precision that read as considered work worth a teacher's attention.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as the baseline: body text contrast ≥4.5:1, readable type sizes, and full reduced-motion support for any animation. No additional stated requirements.
